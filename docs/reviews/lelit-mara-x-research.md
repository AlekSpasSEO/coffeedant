# Lelit Mara X PL62X research ledger

## Scope and method

This ledger supports `/espresso-machine/lelit-mara-x/` and permanent product ID `CD-EM-000203`. The review is research-led. Coffeedant did not operate or bench-test a unit. Current first-party records control identity and specifications; independent testing, owner reports and retail listings are labelled by role.

Checked September 24, 2026. The editorial update and sitemap dates are both September 24, 2026.

## Exact identity and generation boundary

The reviewed machine is the current MaraX `PL62X` family, including US article `PESMX02`, with the later operating logic commonly called V2. It is a manual L58E heat-exchanger machine. Early red-logo V1 units use different control behavior.

The separate MaraX3 `PL62X3` adds Lelit’s Pagaia flow-control system and is outside scope. Retailers use “V3” inconsistently for PL62X, so the review does not adopt that label as an official generation. Rating plate, article code, voltage, finish, region and manual control identity.

## Current official facts

- L58E lever group with mechanical pre-infusion.
- 1.8 L AISI 316L stainless heat exchanger.
- Double-probe control of the heat exchanger and steam-boiler state.
- Thermosyphon circulation and manufacturer-described silent pump.
- Xmode Coffee and Xmode Steam.
- Warm, Hot and Extra Hot range selector.
- Post-extraction steam boost, standby and reserve mode.
- Dual pressure manometer.
- Spring-closure taps and cool-touch steam and hot-water wands.
- 2.5 L current official tank record.
- 22.5 × 52 × 35.5 cm and 18.5 kg.
- Current regional records include 120 V 60 Hz and several 220-240 V or 230 V articles.

The exact manual says 2.6 L for the tank while the current product page and technical sheet say 2.5 L. The review preserves that conflict.

## Operating modes and care

Lelit’s extended guide says Xmode Steam is the factory setting. It prioritizes steam and suits milk drinks but provides less stable brew behavior. Xmode Coffee turns off the heating element during extraction to reduce overheating and improve thermal stability. The guide suggests nominal 90°C, 92°C and 94°C ranges for Warm, Hot and Extra Hot.

The steam boost follows a lever cycle and is specified to hold more than 1.2 bar for up to 120 seconds. The manufacturer states a maximum readiness time of about 24 minutes. These are manufacturer specifications, not Coffeedant measurements.

The exact manual covers daily group, tray and wand care, weekly detergent backflushing and annual specialist descaling. The extended guide recommends replacing its resin filter after 35 L and within four months and keeping chloride below 30 ppm. Local water and current regional service guidance still govern treatment.

## Price and availability

## Independent evidence

Kaffeemacher says it independently purchased and tested the machine and used multiple units in courses. Its two measurement series differed only by flush routine. A brief flush after extraction, about two minutes before the next shot and at least 15 minutes of stabilization after a long session produced its most coherent result. It measured approximate ranges of 90-91°C, 91-92°C and 93-95°C across the three selector positions in its setup. Its group thermometer read 1-2°C above basket delivery. All numbers belong to Kaffeemacher’s machines, instruments and sequence.

Coffee Chronicler published a one-year review of a privately purchased V2 on April 22, 2025. The reviewer liked build, accessories and steam but reported serious temperature problems and confusing mode documentation. His thermocouple indicated roughly 98-100°C on medium. That is important conflicting evidence from one unit, not a fleet result.

The review does not average the two sources into a false universal temperature. It explains the conflict and directs persistently abnormal behavior to qualified service.

## Editorial conclusion

MaraX is a genuinely considered heat exchanger. Xmode Coffee changes heater behavior during extraction, the cabinet is narrow, and Xmode Steam gives milk drinkers useful capacity. It is not automatic temperature control in the dual-boiler sense. Independent evidence shows that post-shot flushing, spacing and recovery still matter, and one privately owned unit ran much hotter than another test set. The right buyer wants compact E61 feel and accepts one stable thermal routine.
