# Lelit Victoria PL91T research ledger

## Scope and method

This ledger supports `/espresso-machine/lelit-victoria-pl91t/` and permanent product ID `CD-EM-000204`. The review is research-led. Coffeedant did not operate or bench-test a unit. Current first-party records control identity and specifications; independent tests, owner reports and retail listings are labelled by role.

Checked September 24, 2026. The editorial update and sitemap dates are both September 24, 2026.

## Exact identity and generation boundary

The reviewed machine is the current conventional Victoria `PL91T` family, including US `PL91T-120` / `PESVI01`. It is a PID-controlled 58 mm single-boiler manual machine.

The Victoria 3 shown at World of Coffee Brussels in July 2026 is outside scope. Kaffeemacher reported that the new machine adds electronic Pagaia flow control, a pressure sensor and two programmable profile buttons, but had not tested it. Current official PL91T records remain active. Rating plate, article code, voltage, region and exact manual govern identity.

Early and later PL91T software also differ. A manufacturer-branded software guide documents the change from a countdown to an ascending shot chronometer, reserve-mode behavior, dose counters and default settings. The review avoids assigning later behavior to an unidentified early unit.

- One multidirectional steam and hot-water wand with anti-burn handle.

The current product page lists a 7-9 g IMS single basket, while the technical sheet says 9-11 g. Earlier manufacturer documentation reports 22.5 × 27 × 38 cm and 9.2 kg. Kaffeemacher measured 23.5 cm wide including the knob, 27.8 cm deep before the plug, 38 cm high, 10.9 kg and 2.35 L. The review preserves these differences rather than averaging them.

## Independent evidence

Kaffeemacher says it purchases all test equipment independently. Its conventional PL91T reached the displayed brew state after about three minutes but needed about 23 minutes for full normal thermal stability. Its accelerated routine used seven minutes in steam mode, a return to brew, a blank shot and a one-minute pause. That allowed one useful espresso but did not make the machine continuously stable.

Across a 14-shot WBC protocol, Kaffeemacher recorded a 1.28°C standard deviation. It also found a significant offset between the selected temperature and the measured result. These findings support repeatability on one unit while warning that the setpoint may not be accurate. A qualified dealer should check the specific machine rather than applying a universal correction.

Kaffeemacher reported about 90 seconds from coffee to steam readiness and 65 seconds to heat water in a 0.6 L pitcher. It viewed steam as adequate for occasional cappuccino and the fixed pre-brewing routine as unsuitable for good extraction because the pre-brew water drains.

Coffee Kev’s independent review used a different unit and protocol. It reported roughly 90 seconds to steam readiness, about 35 to 36 seconds to heat 170 ml of milk from cold to 140°F once ready, and about 35 seconds to return to brew readiness with the steam valve open. It describes the enabled preinfusion as roughly three seconds of pump flow, a three-second pause, then normal flow. These observations are attributed, not universal specifications.

CoffeeGeek’s November 13, 2024 first look adds independent control, construction and workflow context. It is not a long-term reliability study.

## Editorial conclusion

Victoria PL91T is a complete espresso-first single boiler. The LCC, timer, 58 mm group, three-way valve, gauge and large tank solve useful daily problems in a compact chassis. The buyer should keep two limits visible: milk drinks require a state change of roughly 90 seconds in independent testing, and one rigorous test found a meaningful temperature offset despite good repeatability. It is strongest when espresso is the priority and occasional milk is enough.
