Counts strip HTML tags and include citation labels. They exclude title, standfirst, table, FAQs and source notes. The JSON was parsed successfully with PowerShell `ConvertFrom-Json`.

All article paragraphs are original prose. No historical source is quoted directly. Source links appear near factual claims; interpretations are expressly identified where helpful. ## Chronology and visual constraints

1. The first opening claim concerns **Istanbul**, around 1554–55 in Peçevi's retrospective chronology, with competing 1552–53 accounts. Do not change it to the Ottoman Empire's first coffeehouse.
2. Süleyman's reign supplies context; no evidence here establishes a royal founding or an opening decree.
3. The Red Sea route visual is a **reconstructed regional network**. It is not the known route of an individual 1554 consignment. In particular, the two named proprietors' home cities do not establish cargo waypoints.
4. Mocha's principal entrepôt role belongs to the early seventeenth century. Detailed Danish observations of mountain–Beit al-Fakih–port movements belong to **1762–63**. Do not project either backward onto 1554.
5. Arabica's native range, Yemen's agricultural history, early Sufi beverage traditions and Istanbul's shop chronology answer different origin questions.
6. UNESCO's Turkish coffee description concerns a living practice inscribed in 2013. It is not evidence of a precise sixteenth-century brew recipe. Its Khawlani cultivation description concerns contemporary Saudi Arabian practices, not an identical early Yemeni method.
7. The London examples span c.1652 and later seventeenth-/eighteenth-century practices. Royal Society exchange and Lloyd's specialization must remain separately dated.
8. Java's compulsory cultivation and Caribbean chattel slavery were different legal/institutional regimes. Both histories involve coercion; the copy does not equate every worker's status.
9. The c.1840 Java statistic is more than 30 percent of all households; the Priangan statistic concerns approximately 65 percent of **landholding** households. Never remove their geography, date or denominator.
10. Any generated historic hero artwork is interpretive. It cannot be captioned as a surviving image, authenticated interior, historic document or evidence of how a particular named proprietor looked.

## Source access notes

- The Turkish Ministry of Culture PDF, Journal of Ottoman Studies PDF, Kafadar PDF on Duke's academic site, Cambridge texts, Courtauld pages, Oxford project and Wellcome catalog were researched through search/open results.
- Some publisher pages restricted direct opens while academic search excerpts remained available; the copy uses only details available in those excerpts, not inaccessible chapter content.
- SALT's 2002 Kahve-Society book supplies an archival discussion of represented coffeehouse interiors. The prose expressly distinguishes those images from a census of actual establishments.
- The URL notes in the JSON clarify what each source supports. Broader regional or chronological claims from sources were not adopted indiscriminately; in particular, questionable conquest/arrival dates in the Ministry book were excluded.
